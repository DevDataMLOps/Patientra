# Phase 3 patient identity resolution contract

## Safety objective

A false merge can attach the wrong clinical history to a person. Phase 3 therefore
optimizes for precision: only uniquely blocked, multiply corroborated, one-to-one
pairs are linked automatically. An uncertain candidate never changes
`patient_master` until an authorized human supplies an explicit `ACCEPT` decision.

## Normalization and candidate generation

Normalization is used only for comparison; source and Silver values are not changed.

- Names and states: Unicode NFKD, ASCII folding, case folding, alphanumeric characters.
- Phones: digits only; Nigerian `0xxxxxxxxxx` and `234xxxxxxxxxx` forms normalize to
  the same 10-digit comparison form.
- Dates: the already-standardized Silver ISO date.

Candidate pairs are cross-hospital only and are generated by at least one controlled
block: exact normalized phone, exact date/sex/state, exact full-name/sex/state, or an
equal-length phone differing by one digit. This avoids an unrestricted Cartesian join.

## Automatic acceptance

A pair is automatically accepted only when all conditions hold:

1. Its normalized phone is exact and occurs once in LG and once in RS.
2. Sex and state agree.
3. Last name agrees.
4. Either date of birth or first name also agrees.
5. Neither record is eligible for another automatic pair.

The decision rules are `UNIQUE_PHONE_DOB_LAST` and
`UNIQUE_PHONE_FULL_NAME`. Phone-only links are prohibited. One-to-many candidates are
never resolved by picking a winner.

## Human review band

Candidates are queued when evidence is substantial but conflicting, including:

- one-digit phone difference with corroborating date or full name;
- exact date, sex, and state with an exact/one-edit name combination;
- exact full name, sex, and state with only birth year or month/day agreeing; or
- a non-unique exact phone with additional corroboration.

Each queue row contains protected local IDs, a pseudonymous case ID, score, evidence
codes, conflict codes, rule version, status, and reviewer fields. It deliberately does
not duplicate names, phones, or dates of birth. Candidate scores explain ordering;
they do not override the acceptance rules.

## Master identifiers and reversibility

Master IDs and case IDs are HMAC-SHA256 tokens derived with a 32-byte-or-longer secret.
The secret is never logged or written to the audit and must remain stable for repeatable
IDs. Hashing is not treated as de-identification: `patient_master`, match decisions,
and the review queue remain access-controlled and Git-ignored.

The crosswalk keeps every local patient ID and its master ID. Match decisions retain
the evidence categories, decision source, rule version, and reviewer provenance. A
link can therefore be reversed by rejecting/correcting the decision and rebuilding
with the same key; source and Silver records are never overwritten.

## Explicit exclusions

Phase 3 creates no readmission labels, admission features, rates, diagnosis analysis,
or model predictions. Those tasks must consume the governed master crosswalk in later
phases.
